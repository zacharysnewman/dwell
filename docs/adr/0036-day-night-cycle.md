# 0036. The day/night cycle: world time drives a keyframed sky, light and fog, as data

- Status: Proposed (built; the owner's review of the look and of the defaults in `DAY_NIGHT.md` §1
  is outstanding)
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Builds on: [0023](0023-bifacial-world.md) (one sky sphere, per-face light),
  [0029](0029-ray-marched-renderer.md) step A1 (the composite pass draws the sky and the fog),
  [0034](0034-sky-clouds.md) (the clouds take their light from the cycle)
- Design: [`DAY_NIGHT.md`](../DAY_NIGHT.md)

## Context

The owner asked for a day/night cycle with good transitions, fog included, and a better sun and
moon (2026-10-15), with a 30-minute day (20 min day, 10 min night) whose length tree growth also
counts in. The sky was one sphere turned as a unit by a client-only angle (a settings slider and
`?skyrot`), with fixed colours: day and night were one gradient across the sphere, and the lights
and fog never changed. World time already existed: the server's tick, saved in the world file and
sent in every snapshot.

## Options considered

- **Time source.** (a) A new protocol field for the time of day, behind a `Welcome` capability flag
  as `RegionEdit` did; (b) the tick already in `Welcome` and every snapshot. (b) needs no protocol
  change and every 0.11 build already sends it.
- **How the look changes.** (a) Keep turning the one sphere and grade it by direction; (b) keyframes
  over the day, interpolated, each setting every colour and light; (c) a physical atmosphere
  (Rayleigh/Mie scattering). (a) can't give a sunset its own colours, a dark but readable night or
  denser fog at dawn; (c) costs more per pixel, is harder to art-direct for a stylised game and
  still needs the lights and fog keyed to it. (b) is cheap, art-directable and data a tool can edit.
- **Interpolation.** Linear in sRGB (corners at keyframes, grey mud between warm and cool),
  smoothstep per segment (stalls at every keyframe), Catmull-Rom (overshoots), or a monotone cubic in
  Oklab (smooth, never overshoots, perceptual).
- **Fog colour.** A keyframed fog colour kept close to the horizon by hand, or the sky's own colour
  in the pixel's direction (already how the composite fogs).

## Decision

- World time is the server's tick; the client derives the time of day from it (no protocol change).
  The clock (day and night seconds, start hour), the sun's path, the sun, moon and star looks and the
  keyframes live in `shared/look/daynight.json`, validated and generated by
  `shared/look/daynight.mjs` into `dwell-look` (CI fails on stale output).
- 14 keyframes keyed by the sun's angle along its path, interpolated by a monotone cubic in Oklab;
  each face's look is the keyframes at its own solar angle (face B's is face A's + 180°).
- The fog's colour stays the sky's colour in the pixel's direction, so it tracks the horizon at
  every time by construction; keyframes vary its density.
- The sky (gradient, horizon band toward the sun, halos) is used for the fog and reflections; the
  sun's disc, the moon (phases, maria, earthshine) and the stars are drawn behind the world only.
  The moon stays opposite the sun.
- The sky rotation setting and `?skyrot`/`?skyspin` are removed; `?time`, `?moon`, `?timespeed`, an
  F3 line and bench steps replace them.

## Consequences

- Every client on a server sees the same time of day, and a world's time resumes with it.
- The clock is now public API: changing the day length or start hour changes the sky on servers of
  the same line, and tree growth will count its days. A per-world day length needs a `Welcome`
  field later.
- Face B has 10 minutes of daylight and 20 of night (the complement of face A's), from the one
  shared sky; a separate sun for face B would need a new model.
- The look is tuned by editing data; a keyframe editor page could edit `daynight.json` directly.
- Composite cost: about 0.1 ms more at 1280×720 on an M-series GPU with the stars on (DAY_NIGHT.md,
  `day-night-cost`).
- Reversal: the old static sky is the noon keyframe everywhere; removing the cycle is replacing the
  keyframes with one.
