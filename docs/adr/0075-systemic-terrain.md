# ADR 0075: Systemic terrain — climate, rock and life as layers, biomes as results

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-10; Phase 57 T1, the rain shadow, built in 0.52.7). Adopts
  [`proposals/SYSTEMIC_TERRAIN.md`](../proposals/SYSTEMIC_TERRAIN.md) with the owner's answers (its §6).
  Extends [ADR 0074](./0074-biomes-and-chisel.md) (biomes and Chisel).
- **Context:** The owner (2026-11-10): biomes are "more about meta composition"; the terrain should be
  built systemically — rock types that leave sheer cliffs, a rain shadow behind mountains ("I like the
  rain shadow idea"), tectonic plates for volcanoes and island chains — and Chisel should show what
  emerges at different elevations and climates. Systemic terrain is now the top priority.

## Decision

The terrain is a stack of layers, each data in `shared/worldgen/*.json` checked by the shared
validation (`biomes.mjs`) and generated by `gen.mjs`, each feeding the next (the proposal's §2):
tectonics → world shape → geology (rock types) → climate (temperature, the prevailing wind and its
rain shadow) → weathering and erosion style → soil and life; a "biome" becomes a label and a Chisel
preset for a recognisable combination.

- **Every layer ships neutral** in the default world (owner, §6 q2): its default data leaves the terrain
  a seed generates unchanged (the worldgen goldens byte-identical), and it is tuned in Chisel first.
  The tuned layers are adopted together on one new compatibility line.
- **Order** (owner, §6 q1, q3, q4): T1 the rain shadow; T2 rock provinces and rock types; T3 soil and
  vegetation from climate (tree line, snow line); T4 rule-based erosion shaping per rock (caprock
  terraces, cliff faces, scree), with a simulated erosion pass to compare later; T5 tectonics at real
  scale (one plate boundary across the island, hotspots offshore making island chains). Volcanoes
  dormant or active is still open (§5 q5).

## T1, the rain shadow (built, 0.52.7)

`terrain.json` `biomeMap` gains the climate keys (one group with the biome map's other geography
inputs, so Chisel's sliders, state and parsing carry them): `windDirection` (degrees the wind blows
from, face-local), `windReach` (m of full rain that dry the air), `baseRain` (share rained per km on
flat land), `upliftRain` / `leeDry` (more rain per 10 % grade the ground rises along the wind, less
per 10 % it falls) and `rainShare` (the rainfall's share of a biome's wetness; **0 in the default
world**, so the wetness is the rivers' and lakes' alone and nothing changes).

`biomemap::rainfall` marches the wind across each face's world map once per biome field (cells in
upwind order, each taking the air two cells upwind by bilinear sampling, the sea refilling it): rain
= moisture × intensity (0..1), the air losing that rain × step / `windReach`. One pass over the
~500² map, O(n² log n) for the order; Chisel's 16 km preview stays under 0.1 s per tile. Chisel adds
the rainfall overlay and sliders for the keys. Tests: a ridge fixture wetter windward than lee,
bounded, the air losing exactly what rained; each face's map of seed 7 with a strong wind, windward
slopes > 2× the lee's rain; `rainShare` 0 leaves the geography identical whatever the wind; the
goldens.

### Follow-up: the regional shadow (0.52.8)

T1's first look was dominated by per-slope striping: at the map's 100 m cells the intensity saturates
near a 7 % grade, so each hillside facing the wind took full rain and a whole range's lee read weakly.
The wind now sees the ground low-passed over `windSmoothM` m (`biomeMap`, 3 km by default; a tent of
two box passes, `biomemap::smooth_ground`; 0 is the raw map), so rain follows ranges. Still neutral
(`rainShare` 0). Test: behind a broad range with ±25 m hillsides, every lee cell for 2.3 km is drier
than half the windward flank's mean; on the raw map a lee hillside is not.

## Presets (built, 0.52.8)

The owner (2026-11-10): "We need a lot of prefabbed biomes as options to use as starting points." A
folder of preset biomes, `shared/worldgen/biomes/presets/` (14, prototype content on today's blocks):
the biome format plus a `label` and a `description` (optional for any biome, required of a preset),
validated by the same rules (`biomes.mjs` `validatePresets`), emitted as `PRESET_BIOMES` for tests,
listed by Chisel ("Add from preset"), and never in the default world's biome list (the goldens
unchanged). Each sets a cohesive `where` (the desert warm and dry, the tundra high and cold, the beach
low and coastal, the marsh wet and low, …), its bands, relief, banks and water look. Rock types (T2)
and climate-driven soil (T3) will let the canyon and badlands presets read as their own rock; today
stone stands in.

## T2, rock types and shaping (built, 0.52.9)

`shared/worldgen/rocks.json` (checked by `biomes.mjs` `validateRocks`, emitted as `rocks.gen.rs`):
**rock types** (hardness 0..1; jointing `bedded` | `vertical` | `massive`; bed thickness; solubility,
unused yet; the `face`, `cap` and `scree` blocks; a colour), **stacks** (layers top to bottom, each a rock
and a thickness, repeating with height from sea level; a weight), **provinces** (a noise of `wavelength`
m picks a stack by weight, fading the shaping out at a border) and the **shaping** (`strength`, 0 in the
default world; `tread`, `sheer`). A biome's optional `rockStack` takes the province's place (the red-rock
mesa preset `dwell:red_beds`, sandstone over shale; badlands `dwell:badland_beds`, thin sandstone over
thick shale).

The shaping is rule-based (the owner's §6 q3): each stack is a monotone piecewise-linear profile over its
period: a hard bed rises over (1 − hardness)^sheer of its thickness of input height (a near-vertical wall)
and then holds over hardness² × tread of it (a flat tread: caprock); a soft bed rises over more (talus);
vertical jointing makes one wall the unit's height (×¼ the input); massive rock rises unstepped. The
column's ground moves `strength` of the way to the profile's height, so the change is bounded by a period,
blended by the biomes' weights and faded at the sea, rivers and lakes. Where the shaping is at least half
strength, a wall shows its rock's face block, talus its scree, a tread its cap (per-rock palettes after the
biomes'). Face-local (it maps the height over the sea), so both faces. Chisel adds a Rock group of sliders
and a rock overlay.

First look (Chisel, the mesa preset at 0.9): the ground reads as contour terraces — stone caps and walls,
sand talus — of stepped hills, not Monument Valley's isolated buttes on a flat floor: a height-only rule
steps every contour. Isolated buttes need the erosion's plan view (T4), which keeps the simulated pass to
compare; massive rock is unstepped, not yet domed.

## T3, soil and vegetation from climate (built, 0.52.14)

`shared/worldgen/vegetation.json` (checked by `biomes.mjs` `validateVegetation`, emitted as
`vegetation.gen.rs`; `dwell-worldgen` `vegetation.rs`): the **climate in °C** (the biome map's
temperature, which already falls by `lapseRate` per km, × `climate.perUnitC` + `zeroC`: 0.3/km × 21.7 ≈
6.5 °C/km), **soil** per rock (`rockSoils`: a depth on flat ground and a soil type of `soilTypes`; a rock
not listed takes `fallback`), thinning to 0 at `soil.flatSlopeDeg` and deepened by `soil.valley` × the
wetness on valley floors, and **vegetation kinds**: each gives one placement (`placements.json`, by name)
a climate envelope: a temperature range (°C), a moisture range (the biome map's wetness: the rivers, and
T1's rainfall by `rainShare`), a least soil depth, a steepest slope, optional soil types, each edge faded
over `fade`, times `density`. A biome's `vegetation` (0..1, absent = 0) is the climate's share of its
placements: the factor on a candidate's chance is 1 − s + s × the kind's density (`Vegetation::factor`),
asked only of a candidate its other rolls keep (`placements::trees_in_with`) and of the LOD's canopy tint
(`Planter::canopy_at_with`). So the **tree line** emerges where the temperature falls below a kind's least
(the build's conifers: 6.5 °C, the growing-season ≈ 6–7 °C), and a **dry lee** has no trees. The default
biome has none (and a world of one biome builds no biome map, so no climate): the default world and the
goldens are byte-identical. The boreal, cold rainforest, savanna, sand desert and alpine tundra presets
opt in (`vegetation: 1`; savanna and tundra now list the pine groves, which the climate thins). Chisel:
a "Soil and vegetation" group (the °C scale, the soil rules, each kind's tree-line temperature and least
moisture), a per-biome vegetation slider, soil and vegetation overlays; the edit may carry `vegetation`.

Not yet: the soil changes no block (the dirt's depth is still the bands' `underDepth`), the snow line
is still the bands' fixed height (a temperature-driven snow line is T3b), decorations (grass) and ground
items are not governed by kinds yet, and the climate needs the biome map (two or more biomes).

First look (Chisel, seed 7, the default and boreal both at 1, `rainShare` 0.7): trees follow the river
corridors and the wetter windward ground; with the moisture floor at 0 and a 9 °C tree line, the
vegetation overlay thins with altitude and on thin granite soil. At the default rain field the
rainfall is small (mostly 0–0.15), so the river wetness dominates the moisture: the rain shadow reads
weakly until `baseRain`/`rainShare` are tuned. The blocks view at 64 m texels shows little canopy (the
canopy tint is faint at that zoom).

## Consequences

- The biome map's wetness can be climate-driven; a desert can now follow a rain shadow rather than a
  noise. The default world is unchanged until the layers are adopted on a new line.
- The wind is per world (data), not from the seed; a seed-chosen wind can be a later key.
