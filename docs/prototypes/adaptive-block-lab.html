The steepest a natural surface may get. At 27° a lone block stands ¼ m tall; at 45° and steeper, ½ m.

How deep the surface may cut. With one block, a 2-block drop leaves a small wall; with two, it becomes a slope.

The rule

  1. Rest. Each corner of a top block looks at the 3 columns around it. If none is higher or lower, it sits at half height.
  2. Drop. If any of them is lower, the corner drops to the block's floor.
  3. Rise. If one is higher and none is lower, the corner rises to the block's top.
  4. One block wide. When every corner drops, a half-height ridge runs toward each same-height neighbour: half pyramid, ridge end, ridge, ridge corner, T or cross.
  5. Ramp up. When every corner drops and exactly one neighbour across an edge is higher (none the same height), the block is a wedge rising to it.
  1. Every voxel is a cell of the grid. A natural voxel with another on top is a full cell.
  2. A natural top voxel's surface is 9 points: corners, edge midpoints, centre. A corner drops to the voxel's floor when at least 2 of the 3 voxels around it are lower; an edge midpoint drops when the voxel across is lower and a corner of that edge drops. Otherwise points stay at the top.
  3. A point touching both a higher and a lower neighbour stays at the top, so a voxel at a step becomes a ramp up to the higher one.
  4. So a lone voxel is a pyramid, a row is a ridge, a 1-voxel step is a wedge, and nothing leaves its own cell. Bigger drops are plain walls.
  1. Every column has a top. A natural top block with nothing on it rests at half height (top − ½); a block with another on it is a full cube.
  2. Each top is a 3 × 3 grid of points: corners, edge midpoints and centre. A point shared by columns takes a height all of them can reach: where a half-height top meets a step up, that is the top of the lower block, so terraces join without a wall. Beside open ground it is the ground.
  3. Within a block, heights change no faster than the slope: first nothing rises above a lower point + slope × distance, then nothing falls below a higher point − slope × distance. A block between open ground and a higher block becomes a ramp up to it.
  4. The surface stays inside the column's top block, or its top two with Shape depth set to 2. Where neighbours share no height (a pillar's side), a vertical wall carries the difference.
