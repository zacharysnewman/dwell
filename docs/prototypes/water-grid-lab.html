Water Grid Lab

Cellular water: each voxel (1 × ½ × 1 m) holds a fraction of water and passes it down, sideways and, when pressed over full, up. Fully 3D: caves, pipes, overhangs, falls. Springs (cyan) hold their cell full; sinks (the edge) remove water.

Surface grid: Dwell's candidate water model: a depth on each 1 m ground column, moving to its neighbours across their shared faces (shallow-water flow: it needs depth to move). Springs hold their cell full; water reaching the far edge is removed. Ground steps are ½ m.

Water graph: the ground is cut into pools (flats: a lake with its rim, a river's stretch between two steps, a bank; cut into 4 m pieces by default), each holding a volume, its level from that volume. Each face between two pools carries a discharge by the grid's own law (pushed by the pools' levels, slowed by friction, through the water over the crest, capped at critical flow): a fall where the lower pool is below the crest (flowing water, drawn as curtains), a drowned crest where both stand over it. Only volumes and face discharges are stepped; water is conserved exactly. "Every column" makes it the grid.

Steady solve: no water is stepped through time. From the ground and the springs' discharge (m³/s), a priority-flood finds every column's spill level, a lake's crest (orange) and where each column drains; the discharge is summed downhill; a lake stands at its crest plus the head its outflow needs, a stream at the depth that carries its flow. A dry pit stays dry. Edits re-solve at once (the change is eased in, not simulated).
